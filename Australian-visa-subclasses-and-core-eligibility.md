# Australian visa subclasses: inventory and core eligibility

**Research date:** 2 October 2026  
**Inventory source:** [Department of Home Affairs - Visa list](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing), shown as last updated 25 September 2026.

## Scope and limitations

**Boxed UI view:** [Open the styled, one-subclass-per-box view](Australian-visa-subclasses-and-core-eligibility.html). Each box is a single table row; scroll horizontally on narrow screens.

This guide covers the visa entries in the six non-repealed sections of the Department's current Visa list. It records **74 distinct subclass numbers across 68 linked entries**, plus the separate Special Purpose visa entry that has no subclass number. The distinct subclass numbers are also listed in the completeness check near the end.

The listing includes some visa subclasses with closed, limited, or transitional application pathways. Their presence here does **not** mean new applications are open. Check the linked Department page and current application instructions before acting.

Each row identifies the usual visa outcome (temporary, provisional, or permanent residence) and whether an applicant needs a sponsor, nominator, proposer, or other pathway support. “No visa sponsor” does not remove eligibility, invitation, nomination, enrolment, or evidence requirements. The description / purpose column is a brief orientation summary. The criteria are presented as bullets and summarize the main published eligibility tests; they are not a complete legal checklist or a guarantee of eligibility. Stream-specific rules, age limits, points, occupation lists, sponsorship approvals, invitation rounds, health/character/security criteria, and location-at-application rules can change or vary by applicant. The linked Department page is authoritative.

**Important capture limitation:** the Department's edge service returned `403 Access Denied` for 12 detail-page URLs during this capture. Those URLs and subclass numbers are still included in the inventory so none are omitted. For those entries, purpose descriptions are high-level orientation based on the official listing label, and detailed criteria are explicitly marked as unverified rather than guessed. The visa-list inventory itself was accessible.

**Status labels:** “Permanent residence (PR)” means the visa grants permanent residence if granted; “Temporary — provisional” is a temporary visa/pathway that may lead to a later permanent stage; bridging and special-purpose authorities do not themselves grant PR. “Sponsor / application route” distinguishes a formal sponsor, nominator, proposer, nomination, ballot, or self-application. A sponsor is not required for every visitor-visa stream.


**Table layout:** the following is one continuous table with one row per subclass. Visa category, status, sponsor/application route, purpose, eligibility, and official detail are separate columns. The separate unnumbered Special Purpose entry is included as its own row.
